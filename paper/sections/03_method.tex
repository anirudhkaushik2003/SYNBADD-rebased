We address the problem of synthesizing a missing brain MRI scan at an intermediate timepoint in a subject's longitudinal imaging sequence by modelling anatomical deformations over time. Consider a subject with scans acquired at discrete timepoints $\{T_0, T_1, \dots, T_n\}$, where $T_0$ represents the baseline or initial screening scan obtained upon the subject's enrollment in the study and $T_i$ represents the scan at $i^{th}$ timepoint. Let $T_p \in \{T_1, \dots, T_n\}$ denote the timepoint at which the scan is missing. Each available scan $I_{T_i}$ is registered to the baseline $I_0$ using symmetric diffeomorphic registration via ANTs toolkit~\cite{}. This produces a stationary velocity field (SVF), denoted $\phi_{0 \rightarrow T_i}$, which encodes the deformation from the baseline to the timepoint $T_i$. These SVFs are used to learn latent representations of anatomical changes in the subject over the given timepoint, which are then used to predict the deformation for the missing timepoint.\\

We aim to estimate the deformation $\hat{\phi}_{0 \rightarrow T_p }$ which corresponds to the SVF from the baseline scan to the missing timepoint $T_p$. The missing image $\hat{I}_{T_p}$ is synthesized by warping the baseline scan $I_0$ as per this predicted deformation. We approach this problem with three key assumptions. First, anatomical changes progress smoothly over time, even though the trajectory may not be strictly linear and may vary across subjects. Second, each deformation can be represented in a low-dimensional latent space that preserves both global structure and local anatomical detail. Third, the neighbouring timepoints of a subject can provide a useful context to infer the deformation at a missing timepoint. To address this, we propose a three-stage framework. We first learn a compact latent representations of the SVFs using a variational Auto-encoder. Next, we use a target-aware temporal transformer to infer the latent deformation at the missing timepoint by attending to relevant information across the available timepoints. Finally, the predicted deformation is refined and applied to the baseline scan to synthesize the missing image in an anatomically consistent manner.

\subsection{Latent Representation Learning of SVFs}

We learn a compact latent representation for each SVF using a Variational Auto-Encoder (VAE) with skip-connections. Each deformation field $\phi_{0 \rightarrow T_i}$ is independently encoded into a latent vector $z_i \in \mathbb{R}^d$ using a convolutional encoder $\mathcal{E}$. In parallel, the encoder also extracts a hierarchy of skip features $s_i$ that preserve spatial detail at multiple resolutions:
\[
z_i, s_i = \mathcal{E}(\phi_{0 \rightarrow T_i})
\]

The decoder $\mathcal{D}$ reconstructs the original SVF from the latent code and the corresponding skip features:
\[
\hat{\phi}_{0 \rightarrow T_i} = \mathcal{D}(z_i, s_i)
\]

The VAE is trained to minimize a loss that includes a reconstruction term between the input and predicted SVF, and a Kullback-Leibler divergence term that regularizes the latent space and EPE and Anglular error loss. This formulation encourages the model to learn smooth, compact embeddings while preserving anatomical detail.

Most subjects only have a few longitudinal scans available which limits the number of training samples. We supplement this by introducing a synthetic SVF augmentation strategy to improve the robsutness of the learned representations. We generate additional augmentations by applying random affine and smooth non-linear transformations to the input fields. These augmentations preserve diffeomorphic properties and introduce variability that helps the model generalize to unseen deformation patterns.

At inference time, the skip features $\{s_j\}_{j \neq p}$ from available timepoints are averaged to create a subject-specific spatial prior:
\[
\bar{s} = \frac{1}{n - 1} \sum_{j \neq p} s_j
\]
This aggregated skip representation is used during decoding of the predicted latent code $\hat{z}_p$ to help preserve anatomical consistency when reconstructing the missing deformation.




\subsection{Trajectory Inference via Target-Aware Temporal Transformer}

Given a sequence of latent representations $\{z_1, \dots, z_n\}$ corresponding to a subject's longitudinal SVFs, our objective is to estimate the latent code $\hat{z}_p$ for a missing timepoint $T_p$. To address this, we propose a target-aware temporal transformer that selectively aggregates information from available timepoints, conditioned on the index of the missing timepoint. Specifically, let $t_p$ denote the index of the missing timepoint. We compute a query embedding $\mathbf{q}_{t_p} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$ using a learnable linear projection $L: \mathbb{Z} \rightarrow \mathbb{R}^d$, such that $\mathbf{q}_{t_p} = L(t_p)$. This embedding is used to guide the attention mechanism within the transformer, allowing the model to focus on temporally relevant latent vectors from the sequence for reconstructing the missing scan.


The input to the transformer consists of latent vectors $\{z_1, \dots, z_n\}$, each representing an observed timepoint. To incorporate temporal order, we add a learnable positional embedding $e_i^{\text{pos}}$:
\[
x_i = z_i + e_i^{\text{pos}}
\]
This augmented sequence $\{x_1, \dots, x_n\}$ is then passed through a transformer encoder, which captures contextual relationships and temporal dynamics across the observed timepoints:

\[
H = \text{Transformer}(\{x_1 \dots x_n\}) \in \mathbb{R}^{T \times D}
\]


% timepoint projection layer assigns weights in the same manner for each subject making it subject agnostic but timepoint specific.

To guide the model’s attention toward more informative timepoints, we apply soft-selection via gated attention. For each token $H_i$, we compute an attention score $a_i = \text{MLP}_{\text{attn}}(H_i)$ and a gating value $g_i = \sigma(\text{MLP}_{\text{gate}}(H_i))$. These are combined to produce a soft attention weight:
\[
\alpha_i = \frac{\exp(a_i \cdot g_i)}{\sum_j \exp(a_j \cdot g_j)}
\]
The resulting gated sequence is obtained by reweighting each token:
\[
\tilde{H}_i = \alpha_i \cdot H_i
\]

To extract a representation specific to the missing timepoint, we use the previously obtained learned embedding $\mathbf{q}_{t_p} = L(t_p)$ associated with the target timepoint's index $t_p$ in the longitudinal sequence. This target-aware query attends to the gated sequence using scaled dot-product attention:
\[
\beta_i = \frac{\exp(q^\top \tilde{H}_i)}{\sum_j \exp(q^\top \tilde{H}_j)}
\]
and produces the pooled representation as a weighted combination of temporal features:
\[
\hat{z}_p = \sum_i \beta_i \tilde{H}_i
\]

By explicitly conditioning both the attention and pooling mechanisms on the target timepoint, we hypothesize the model is able to selectively extract temporal context relevant to the missing deformation. 

% This formulation enables flexible and robust inference across irregular sampling patterns and varying clinical trajectories, producing latent codes that are temporally and anatomically consistent.


\subsection{Image Synthesis with Anatomy-Preserving Warping}

The predicted latent code $\hat{z}_p$ for the missing deformation is decoded using the pretrained decoder $\mathcal{D}$ introduced earlier to reconstruct the SVF. To preserve spatial detail of the subject's morphology in the SVF's representation, we also pass the aggregated skip features $\bar{s}$ computed from the available timepoints to the decoder:
\[
\tilde{\phi}_{0 \rightarrow T_p} = \mathcal{D}(\hat{z}_p, \bar{s})
\]
This results in a coarse estimate of the SVF for the missing timepoint. To refine this estimate and improve anatomical plausibility, we introduce a U-Net–based deformation generator $\mathcal{G}$ that takes the coarse SVF and additional clinical metadata as input. Specifically, we condition the network on the subject’s age, diagnostic label and the target timepoint index using dynamic affine feature transform (DAFT), which injects these variables into the feature maps at multiple layers:
\[
\hat{\phi}_{0 \rightarrow T_p} = \mathcal{G}(\tilde{\phi}_{0 \rightarrow T_p}, \text{age}, \text{label}, t_p)
\]

The final deformation field $\hat{\phi}_{0 \rightarrow T_p}$ is then used to synthesize the missing image by warping the baseline scan $I_0$ using a spatial transformer network (STN):
\[
\hat{I}_{T_p} = \text{STN}(I_0, \hat{\phi}_{0 \rightarrow T_p})
\]

All synthesis operations are performed at the original resolution to ensure fidelity to fine-grained anatomical structures. This completes the pipeline by producing a fully reconstructed image at the target timepoint.

\subsection{Training Objectives}
\label{sec:training_objectives}
 
SynBADD is trained in two stages, each with a dedicated set of loss functions. The first stage trains the variational autoencoder to learn compact latent representations of SVFs. The second stage trains the temporal transformer and spatial refinement module end-to-end for missing scan synthesis.
 
\subsubsection*{Stage 1: VAE Pretraining}
 
The VAE is trained to reconstruct input SVFs while learning a regularised latent space. The objective combines four terms:
 
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{VAE}} = \lambda_{\text{recon}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}} + \lambda_{\text{KL}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}} + \lambda_{\text{EPE}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{EPE}} + \lambda_{\text{ang}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{ang}}
\end{equation}
 
The reconstruction loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{recon}}$ is the mean squared error between the input SVF $\phi_{0 \to T_i}$ and the reconstructed field $\hat{\phi}_{0 \to T_i}$, computed voxel-wise across all three spatial components of the velocity field. The Kullback--Leibler divergence $\mathcal{L}_{\text{KL}}$ regularises the approximate posterior $q(z_i | \phi_{0 \to T_i})$ towards a standard normal prior, encouraging a smooth and compact latent space.
 
To encourage fidelity in both the magnitude and direction of the reconstructed velocity vectors, we include two additional terms. The end-point error $\mathcal{L}_{\text{EPE}}$ measures the average Euclidean distance between the predicted and ground-truth velocity vectors at each spatial location:
 
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{EPE}} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{x \in \Omega} \| \hat{v}(x) - v(x) \|_2
\end{equation}
 
The angular error loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{ang}}$ penalises directional misalignment between predicted and ground-truth vectors, computed as the average angular deviation across spatial locations:
 
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{ang}} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{x \in \Omega} \arccos \left( \frac{\hat{v}(x) \cdot v(x)}{\| \hat{v}(x) \|_2 \, \| v(x) \|_2 + \epsilon} \right)
\end{equation}
 
where $\epsilon$ is a small constant for numerical stability. Together, EPE and angular error ensure that the reconstructed SVF preserves both the scale and orientation of the original deformation field, which is important for maintaining anatomical plausibility in downstream synthesis.
 
\subsubsection*{Stage 2: Refinement and Synthesis}
 
The refinement module $G$ and the spatial transformer network are trained jointly using a composite objective that combines deformation-level and image-level losses:
 
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{synth}} = \lambda_{\text{def}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{def}} + \lambda_{\text{SSIM}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{SSIM}} + \lambda_{\text{MSE}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} + \lambda_{\text{Jac}} \mathcal{L}_{\text{Jac}}
\end{equation}
 
The deformation energy loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{def}}$, inspired by LDDMM formulations~[], encourages smooth, diffeomorphic deformation fields by penalising excessive deformation energy:
 
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{def}} = \int_{\Omega} \| \nabla \hat{\phi}_{0 \to T_p}(x) \|^2 \, dx
\end{equation}
 
This term acts as a spatial regulariser, discouraging high-frequency deformation artifacts and promoting smoothly varying velocity fields.
 
The structural similarity loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{SSIM}} = 1 - \text{SSIM}(\hat{I}_{T_p}, I_{T_p})$ promotes perceptual consistency between the synthesised scan $\hat{I}_{T_p}$ and the ground-truth target $I_{T_p}$, while the mean squared error loss $\mathcal{L}_{\text{MSE}} = \| \hat{I}_{T_p} - I_{T_p} \|^2$ enforces voxel-level intensity agreement. These two terms operate on the final warped image rather than on the deformation field itself, providing direct supervision on the synthesised output.
 
The Jacobian determinant regularisation $\mathcal{L}_{\text{Jac}}$ constrains the local volume change induced by the predicted deformation to remain within a biologically plausible range:
 
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{Jac}} = \frac{1}{|\Omega|} \sum_{x \in \Omega} \left( \det(J_{\hat{\phi}}(x)) - \tau \right)^2
\end{equation}
 
where $\det(J_{\hat{\phi}}(x))$ is the Jacobian determinant of the predicted deformation at location $x$, and $\tau = 0.9$ is the target value. A target slightly below unity reflects the mild tissue contraction expected in a cohort that includes subjects undergoing neurodegenerative progression, where average atrophy rates in the range of 0.3--5\% per year have been reported depending on clinical stage~[]. This regularisation discourages both excessive shrinkage and implausible tissue expansion, anchoring the predicted deformations to physiologically realistic patterns.
 
The loss weights are set as $\lambda_{\text{def}} = 0.5$, $\lambda_{\text{SSIM}} = 0.05$, $\lambda_{\text{MSE}} = 0.05$, and $\lambda_{\text{Jac}}$ is tuned on the validation set. The higher weight on the deformation energy term reflects its importance in maintaining diffeomorphic properties, while the image-level losses provide complementary supervision on the final output.
