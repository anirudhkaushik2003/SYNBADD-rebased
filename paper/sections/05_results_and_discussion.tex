\section{Results and Discussion}

% 5.1 Evaluation of SynBADD Variants
% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Evaluation of SynBADD Variants}
\label{sec:ablation}

We analyse the contribution of each module introduced in SynBADD through a series of controlled ablation experiments. The evaluation is organised along two axes: the design of the temporal transformer that operates in the latent space, and the spatial refinement stage that operates on the decoded SVF. All results are averaged over five-fold cross-validation.

% ----------------------------------------------------------
% 5.1.1 Effect of Temporal Transformer Design
% ----------------------------------------------------------

\subsubsection{Effect of Temporal Transformer Design}

% We use 3 variants of the transformer's cross attention architecture and the results are displayed in Table \ref{tab:transformer variants}. A gated attention variant was evaluated in which a learned sigmoid gate scales per-token attention scores, enabling the model to suppress noisy inputs and emphasize more informative time points when forming the sequence representation. In the other variant, cross-attention is applied first to identify time points relevant to the missing index, after which a gating mechanism modulates these contributions to attenuate noisy or unreliable signals. The introduction of the gating module helps determine whether the model leverages the full longitudinal signal as gating would help in minimizing noisy signals from far-away timepoints.

% Table \ref{tab:transformer variants} indicates similar performance across all variants with minor improvements in PSNR for when Gating is applied after cross attention. SSIM for the base variant is the highest overall.

We compare three variants of the cross-attention mechanism used to aggregate temporal information from the observed latent codes. In the base variant, cross-attention is applied directly, using the target-aware query to pool over the encoded sequence. In the second variant, a learned sigmoid gate is introduced to scale per-token attention scores before pooling, enabling the model to suppress contributions from less informative timepoints. In the third variant, the order is reversed: gating is applied first to modulate the encoded tokens, after which cross-attention is used to aggregate the gated representations with respect to the target query.

Table~\ref{tab:attention} reports the results. Performance is broadly similar across all three variants, with PSNR values ranging from 33.39 to 33.49 and SSIM remaining stable around 0.9882--0.9883. A minor improvement in PSNR is observed when gating is applied after cross-attention, suggesting that post-hoc suppression of noisy signals may offer a slight advantage over pre-filtering. However, the base cross-attention variant achieves the highest SSIM overall.

The consistency across variants indicates that the transformer architecture is relatively robust to the specific form of the attention mechanism. This is likely because the latent codes, having been extracted from a pretrained VAE, already constitute a compact and structured representation in which the temporal signal is well-separated from noise. In the subsequent experiments, we adopt the base cross-attention variant for its simplicity and marginally stronger structural similarity.

\subsubsection{Effect of SVF Refinement}

The SVF decoded from the predicted latent code provides a coarse estimate of the deformation field at the missing timepoint. To assess whether spatial refinement improves this estimate, we compare three configurations: (i) the full model with U-Net refinement and clinical conditioning via DAFT, (ii) refinement with U-Net alone, and (iii) no refinement module.

Table~\ref{tab:refinement} summarises the results. Introducing the U-Net refinement module improves PSNR by 1.90\% over the variant without any refinement, with a corresponding increase in SSIM from 0.9857 to 0.9876. Adding DAFT conditioning on top of the U-Net yields a further improvement, bringing PSNR to 33.42 and SSIM to 0.9883. The cumulative effect of the full refinement pipeline amounts to a PSNR gain of 3.16\% relative to the no-refinement baseline.

These results confirm that the coarse SVF reconstructed from the latent code, while capturing the overall trajectory of deformation, lacks the spatial precision required to faithfully represent fine-grained anatomical structures. The refinement module operates at full resolution and adjusts local deformation patterns that the low-dimensional bottleneck cannot preserve. We also observed that the qualitative alignment with target anatomy improves noticeably with the full refinement pipeline, particularly in regions exhibiting complex cortical folding such as the temporal and parietal lobes. This improvement is not fully reflected in PSNR and SSIM, which are global metrics and may underweight localised gains in anatomically critical areas.

\subsubsection{Impact of Clinical Conditioning}

In the full model, the U-Net refinement module is conditioned via DAFT using a 3-dimensional auxiliary vector composed of diagnostic group, subject age, and the target timepoint encoding. Concretely, the conditioning input is formed as $x_{\mathrm{aux}} = [\mathrm{group},\; \mathrm{age},\; t_{\mathrm{target}}]$, where group is encoded as CN\,=\,0, MCI\,=\,1, and AD\,=\,2. This design allows the refinement network to modulate predicted deformations according to both subject-specific disease stage and the temporal horizon of the synthesis task. Age provides a strong prior on the rate of neurodegeneration, as it directly influences baseline brain morphology and the expected trajectory of volumetric decline. Diagnostic label encodes disease-stage information that can modulate both the magnitude and spatial distribution of predicted deformations. The target timepoint encoding enables the network to scale deformation magnitude to the requested longitudinal horizon.

From Table~\ref{tab:refinement}, DAFT conditioning provides an additional PSNR improvement of approximately 1.24\% over the U-Net--only variant, with a corresponding marginal increase in SSIM. The quantitative gain from conditioning is moderate in global metrics, which is not unexpected given that age and diagnosis are scalar-valued signals being injected into a high-dimensional spatial field. To assess whether the benefit of conditioning is anatomically localised, Table~\ref{tab:model_variant_dice} reports Dice overlap across eight regions of interest that are highly relevant to Alzheimer's disease progression.

The U-Net--only variant performs worst across all evaluated ROIs, indicating that spatial refinement without auxiliary conditioning can degrade regional boundary accuracy despite improving global image fidelity (PSNR/SSIM). In contrast, adding DAFT conditioning substantially recovers this loss and yields the best mean Dice across regions (0.9578), with the highest scores in 5 of 8 ROIs: entorhinal cortex, fusiform gyrus, inferior temporal gyrus, middle temporal gyrus, and third ventricle.

The largest gains of U-Net+DAFT over the U-Net--only variant are observed in the inferior temporal gyrus (+0.054), middle temporal gyrus (+0.065), entorhinal cortex (+0.033), and fusiform gyrus (+0.030), with a smaller gain in the third ventricle (+0.010). This pattern is anatomically meaningful: Alzheimer's disease is known to affect the medial temporal lobe early---including the entorhinal cortex and hippocampal system---before extending into broader inferior and middle temporal neocortical regions, while ventricular enlargement is a well-established macroscopic structural marker of disease progression~\cite{braak1991neuropathological,jack2018nia}. The clustering of DAFT gains in these structures suggests that conditioning on disease group, age, and target timepoint helps the model generate anatomically more plausible deformations in regions that are especially sensitive to neurodegenerative change.

Interestingly, the no-refinement baseline remains marginally superior in the hippocampus, amygdala, and parahippocampal region. A plausible explanation is that the refinement network, when left unconditioned, can slightly perturb already-good coarse deformations around small, tightly folded medial temporal structures where Dice is sensitive to even minor boundary shifts. DAFT conditioning recovers much of this loss but does not fully exceed the baseline in those three ROIs. This is consistent with the global results: adding the U-Net alone improves PSNR and SSIM, but the ROI analysis reveals that this improvement is not uniformly distributed across structures. The refinement module helps globally but can be locally heavy-handed unless guided by subject-specific metadata.

Taken together, the ablation results confirm that each component of SynBADD contributes to overall performance. The temporal transformer provides a stable mechanism for aggregating longitudinal context, the refinement module recovers spatial detail lost during latent decoding, and clinical conditioning introduces subject-specific priors that concentrate improvements in disease-relevant anatomy. The Dice analysis complements the PSNR and SSIM results by showing that the benefit of DAFT is not merely global but is concentrated in anatomical regions implicated in Alzheimer's disease progression.

\subsection{Comparison with State-of-the-Art Methods}
\label{sec:sota}

% As each scan is 128x128x128 and the subject's brain on average only occupies ~50\% of the scan volume, there is a requirement to get rid of noisy components for a fair evaluation across methods. We use brain-masking to extract the brain and compute the metrics on this masked region. NODER and ReMiND were evaluated on separate test sets, with the set for ReMiND being a subset of NODER as ReMiND requires both the immediate succeeding and immediate preceeding scans to be present whereas NODER does not have such a constraint. We benchmark SynBADD in both the scenarios using the same test set as we used for evaluating the other model. We also compare with a baseline method of imputation, \textbf{Forward Difference} where the closest previous timepoint is used as a substitute for the target timepoint.

We benchmark SynBADD against two recent methods for missing scan imputation in longitudinal brain MRI: NODER~\cite{bai2024noder}, a neural ODE--based framework that learns subject-specific deformation trajectories through curve fitting in SVF space, and ReMiND~\cite{yuan2024remind}, a diffusion model that reconstructs a whole-brain volume from generated batches of axial slices in intensity space, conditioned on the immediate preceding and succeeding timepoints. We additionally compare against a simple Forward Difference baseline, in which the closest available preceding scan is used as a substitute for the missing target.

As each scan is of size $128 \times 128 \times 128$ and the brain occupies on average approximately 50\% of the volume, we apply brain-masking to extract the brain region and compute all metrics exclusively on this masked area. This avoids inflation of similarity scores by background voxels and ensures a fair comparison across methods.

NODER and ReMiND impose different requirements on the available temporal context. ReMiND requires both the immediate preceding and immediate succeeding scans to be present, whereas NODER does not enforce such a constraint. Accordingly, the evaluation sets for the two baselines differ: the test set for ReMiND is a subset of that used for NODER. We evaluate SynBADD under both settings, using the same test set as the corresponding baseline in each case, to ensure a fair comparison.

\subsubsection{Comparison based on Image Quality Metrics}

Table~\ref{tab:sota_noder} reports image-level metrics for SynBADD and NODER, averaged over five-fold cross-validation. SynBADD achieves a PSNR of 34.52 compared to 31.40 for NODER, representing an improvement of 9.94\%. SSIM improves from 0.9766 to 0.9912, a gain of 1.50\%. Table~\ref{tab:sota_remind} presents the corresponding comparison with ReMiND. Here the margin is substantially larger: SynBADD achieves a PSNR improvement of 38.18\% and an SSIM improvement of 4.27\% over ReMiND.

The magnitude of the improvement over ReMiND reflects a fundamental difference in generation strategy. ReMiND generates batches of axial slices and reassembles them into a 3D volume; although each batch contains local through-plane context, coherence is not modelled jointly over the complete volume. SynBADD, by contrast, operates natively in 3D deformation space, producing spatially coherent volumes by construction. The improvement over NODER, while smaller in relative terms, is nonetheless substantial and is consistent with SynBADD's ability to leverage population-level learning and bidirectional temporal context, whereas NODER optimises a separate trajectory model for each subject-specific image sequence.

\subsubsection{Comparison on Clinically Relevant metrics}

Beyond global image quality, we evaluate anatomical fidelity by computing Dice coefficients between FreeSurfer parcellations of the synthesised and ground-truth scans across eight brain regions relevant to Alzheimer's disease progression: hippocampus, parahippocampal gyrus, third ventricle, amygdala, entorhinal cortex, fusiform gyrus, inferior temporal gyrus, and middle temporal gyrus.

Table~\ref{tab:dice_noder} shows that SynBADD achieves Dice improvements over NODER ranging from +28.83\% for the third ventricle to +82.55\% for the middle temporal gyrus. The largest gains are observed in regions where NODER produces the weakest overlap with ground truth, such as the entorhinal cortex (0.5168 vs.\ 0.9209) and parahippocampal gyrus (0.5742 vs.\ 0.9612). These regions are affected early in Alzheimer's pathology~\cite{braak1991neuropathological,barnes2009atrophy}, making their anatomical fidelity particularly relevant to longitudinal morphometric analysis.

Table~\ref{tab:dice_remind} presents the comparison with ReMiND. SynBADD achieves consistently high Dice scores across all regions, exceeding 0.96 in every structure. ReMiND, by contrast, produces Dice scores below 0.67 in most regions, with the entorhinal cortex showing the weakest agreement at 0.3742. The gap is particularly pronounced in smaller structures such as the amygdala and entorhinal cortex, where slice-batch synthesis in intensity space struggles to maintain anatomical correspondence.

Table~\ref{tab:dice_forward} and Table~\ref{tab:volume_forward} extend the comparison to the Forward Difference baseline. Table~\ref{tab:dice_forward} shows that SynBADD achieves Dice improvements ranging from +56.30\% for the hippocampus to +110.94\% for the middle temporal gyrus. Table~\ref{tab:volume_forward} reports absolute and relative volumetric differences with respect to ground truth. The Forward Difference approach yields percentage volume errors in the range of 7.23\%--20.34\%, whereas SynBADD reduces these to 1.22\%--3.79\% across all evaluated regions. This indicates that SynBADD not only reconstructs the correct spatial layout of anatomical structures but also preserves their volumetric properties with high fidelity---a property that is essential for downstream tasks such as atrophy rate estimation and biomarker extraction in longitudinal studies.

It is important to interpret the gap over Forward Difference correctly. Forward Difference performs no learning whatsoever: it copies the most recent observed scan into the missing slot. The substantial improvement of SynBADD over this baseline therefore quantifies the value of the entire learned pipeline---deformation-space modelling, the diffeomorphic generator, and the clinical-metadata-conditioned refinement module---rather than the contribution of the temporal transformer in isolation. We return to the question of how much of this gain is attributable to the longitudinal aggregation pathway specifically in Section~\ref{sec:robustness}, where a combined No-UNet$+$No-DAFT ablation suggests that the spatial refinement and conditioning pathway together accounts for the bulk of the observed Dice and SSIM improvements over Forward Difference, while the temporal transformer's contribution is more nuanced.

Figure~\ref{fig:comparison} visualises the Dice comparisons across brain regions, illustrating the consistent advantage of SynBADD over both NODER and ReMiND across all evaluated structures.

\subsubsection{Classification of Comparative Methods}

To contextualise the performance differences reported above, we characterise each method along several design dimensions that influence imputation quality. These are summarised in Table~\ref{tab:method_taxonomy}.

ReMiND operates in the intensity space, generating missing scans in batches of axial slices conditioned on the immediately adjacent timepoints and then reassembling the batches into a volume. Although each batch incorporates local through-plane context, the method does not enforce consistency jointly across the complete volume, nor does it impose explicit anatomical constraints on the generated outputs. Its temporal reasoning is limited to pairwise interpolation between two flanking scans, without access to the broader longitudinal trajectory.

NODER models deformations using stationary velocity fields and learns a continuous trajectory in SVF space via neural ordinary differential equations. However, it requires fitting a separate model for each subject-specific image sequence, which limits scalability and introduces susceptibility to overfitting, particularly when the number of available scans per subject is small. Its supervision is based on image regression, which may lead to oversmoothing of predicted deformations.

SynBADD shares with NODER the principle of operating in deformation space, but differs in several respects. It trains a single population-level model that generalises across subjects and diagnostic groups, rather than requiring per-subject optimisation. It reasons bidirectionally over the full available longitudinal context, rather than relying on pairwise or unidirectional conditioning. The refinement module, conditioned on clinical metadata, provides subject-specific adaptation within the population-level framework. And the synthesis is performed natively in 3D, producing volumetrically coherent outputs by construction.

These design choices collectively account for the observed performance gap. Operating in deformation space preserves anatomical topology, bidirectional attention captures richer temporal context, and population-level training enables robust generalisation even under limited longitudinal sampling.

\subsubsection{Subgroup Analysis Across Diagnostic Groups}

A practical imputation method must perform consistently across diagnostic strata, because subjects with more advanced neurodegeneration exhibit larger and more spatially heterogeneous deformations. We stratify the full evaluation cohort by diagnostic label---cognitively normal (CN), mild cognitive impairment (MCI), and Alzheimer's disease (AD)---and report image quality and deformation-plausibility metrics within each stratum (Table~\ref{tab:subgroup_main}).

The three groups achieve broadly comparable PSNR and SSIM, with a maximum group-wise PSNR spread of 0.28 dB (CN: 33.60, MCI: 33.54, AD: 33.32) and SSIM differences within $1.9 \times 10^{-3}$. The mean Jacobian determinant is slightly higher in AD ($0.9304$) than in CN ($0.9284$) or MCI ($0.9279$), and is lowest---i.e.\ corresponds to the largest contraction---in MCI. This ordering is consistent with the established neurodegeneration trajectory~\cite{braak1991neuropathological,jack2018nia}, in which MCI subjects exhibit the highest rate of tissue loss as they undergo conversion, while AD subjects' atrophy is concentrated in the medial temporal lobe but is more variable globally. Importantly, no negative Jacobian values were observed in any subgroup, confirming that the diffeomorphic property of the predicted deformations is preserved across all clinical strata. The fact that image quality does not degrade in the AD subgroup, despite the larger and more heterogeneous deformations expected in this population, indicates that SynBADD's deformation-based formulation accommodates the demands of advanced neurodegeneration without specialised per-group training.

% 5.3 Robustness to Incomplete Temporal Context
% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Robustness to Incomplete Temporal Context}
\label{sec:robustness}

In clinical practice, subjects rarely present with complete longitudinal imaging sequences. Missed visits, late enrolment, and early dropout frequently result in irregular and sparse temporal sampling. A practical imputation model must therefore degrade gracefully when fewer observations are available, and must handle asymmetric temporal context---where, for instance, more past scans than future scans are accessible, or vice versa. In this section, we evaluate SynBADD's sensitivity to these conditions.

In what follows we conduct two controlled robustness experiments on the held-out subjects of fold 2. In addition to the full SynBADD model with U-Net and DAFT refinement (the configuration used elsewhere in this paper), we include a controlled \textit{No-UNet$+$No-DAFT} ablation that strips both the spatial refinement network and the clinical-metadata conditioning. Comparing the two models allows us to attribute the effect of reduced or asymmetric input context either to the temporal aggregation pathway or to the conditioned spatial refinement stage.

% ----------------------------------------------------------
% 5.3.1 Effect of Reduced Input Timepoints
% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Effect of Reduced Input Timepoints}
\label{sec:reduced_inputs}

\paragraph{Setup.} For each diagnostic group we fix the target as the latest available follow-up that is observed for every subject in that group: m36 for CN and MCI, and m24 for AD (since none of the AD subjects in ADNI-1 have an m36 scan). The set of input timepoints available for masking is then the remaining non-baseline visits in that group's complete cohort: $\{m06, m12, m24\}$ for CN, $\{m06, m12, m18, m24\}$ for MCI, and $\{m06, m12\}$ for AD. We construct configurations $K = 1, 2, \ldots, K_{\max}$ where $K$ denotes the number of input scans retained (always including the screening scan as the source) and inputs are dropped nearest-to-target first. Each configuration is run as a separate forward pass through the trained model, with the masked inputs zeroed and accompanied by zero attention masks. Image-level and Dice metrics are computed per (subject, configuration) pair on the brain-masked region.

\paragraph{Image-level metrics.} Figure~\ref{fig:exp1_image} reports PSNR, SSIM, and SVF endpoint error (EPE) as a function of the number of dropped inputs. The full UNet$+$DAFT model exhibits a striking flat profile: per-group SSIM and PSNR vary by less than $10^{-3}$ and $4 \times 10^{-2}$ dB respectively across the full range of $K$. By contrast, the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT ablation shows a smooth, monotonic decline. EPE in particular grows from $0.18$--$0.33$ at full context to $0.49$--$0.56$ at $K=1$ across the three groups: a $1.5\times$--$2.7\times$ rise in deformation error.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/exp1_image_metrics.pdf}
    \caption{Image-level metrics under reduced input timepoints (Exp.~1). Solid lines: UNet$+$DAFT (full SynBADD); dashed lines: No-UNet$+$No-DAFT ablation. The full model is statistically invariant to the number of available inputs; the ablation degrades smoothly and monotonically.}
    \label{fig:exp1_image}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Anatomical metrics.} Figure~\ref{fig:exp1_dice} reports the average Dice over the eight regions of interest, again as a function of inputs dropped. The full model is essentially flat for all three groups (within-cohort change of $\leq 0.005$ Dice from $K=K_{\max}$ to $K=1$). The No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model shows clean monotonic degradation in MCI ($-0.0059$ Dice from $K_{\max}$ to $K=1$, $p < 10^{-4}$) and AD ($-0.0042$, $p<10^{-2}$), and a smaller non-monotonic effect in CN. The MCI subgroup---which spans the longest evaluation horizon and contains the most heterogeneous trajectories---is the cleanest illustration of the underlying signal.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.95\textwidth]{images/exp1_dice_curves.pdf}
    \caption{Average Dice over the eight ROIs as a function of inputs dropped (Exp.~1). The full UNet$+$DAFT model is invariant; the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT ablation shows monotonic degradation, particularly clear in the MCI cohort which has the longest dynamic range.}
    \label{fig:exp1_dice}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:exp1_perregion} breaks the average down per-region for each diagnostic group. For the full UNet$+$DAFT model the bars at $K{=}\!K_{\max}$ and $K{=}\!1$ are visually indistinguishable in every region. For the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model the $K{=}\!1$ bars are visibly shorter than the $K{=}\!K_{\max}$ bars, with the largest decrements in the temporal-lobe regions (middle and inferior temporal gyrus, fusiform gyrus) and concentrated in MCI.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{images/exp1_per_region_bars.pdf}
    \caption{Per-region Dice at full context vs.\ source-only ($K{=}\!1$) for each diagnostic group (Exp.~1). For the full UNet$+$DAFT model (blue) the two bars coincide for every region, confirming invariance to input availability. For the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model (orange) the $K{=}\!1$ bars are systematically shorter, especially in the temporal-lobe regions.}
    \label{fig:exp1_perregion}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Statistical confirmation.} A non-parametric repeated-measures Friedman test on each group rejects the null of identical medians across $K$ for the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model on every metric ($p < 10^{-4}$ for SSIM, PSNR, EPE, and Dice in MCI). For the UNet$+$DAFT model the only significant Friedman tests are those whose effect size lies at the seventh decimal place ($p \approx 5 \times 10^{-3}$, $\Delta_{\max} \approx 10^{-7}$ for SSIM)---statistically detectable but practically zero. Per-subject Spearman correlations between the number of dropped inputs and the metric show this contrast even more cleanly: for the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model, the per-subject Spearman $\rho$ between dropped-input count and EPE is $+1.0$ for every subject in every group, indicating that EPE rises monotonically with each additional dropped input on a per-subject basis. For the UNet$+$DAFT model the same correlation has mean $\rho = -0.11$ ($p = 0.34$).

A paired Wilcoxon test across the cohort-stable 76 subjects, comparing the per-subject change $\Delta(K_{\max} \to K_{\max}-2)$ between the two models, finds that the two models respond to input reduction in significantly different ways on every meaningful metric: $p = 8.3 \times 10^{-5}$ for SSIM, $p = 9.9 \times 10^{-4}$ for PSNR, $p = 3.6 \times 10^{-14}$ for EPE, and $p = 1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ for average Dice. This cross-model contrast is summarised visually in Figure~\ref{fig:exp1_cross}.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{images/exp1_cross_model_delta.pdf}
    \caption{Cross-model paired-$\Delta$ comparison on the cohort-stable subjects ($n=76$, Exp.~1). Each bar is the mean per-subject change in the metric when two input timepoints are dropped relative to full context. Paired Wilcoxon tests indicate a significant difference between models for every metric: $p=8.3\times10^{-5}$ (SSIM), $p=9.9\times10^{-4}$ (PSNR), $p=3.6\times10^{-14}$ (EPE), and $p=1.1\times10^{-3}$ (average Dice).}
    \label{fig:exp1_cross}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The contrast between the two models suggests that the apparent robustness of the full model is associated with the combined U-Net refinement and DAFT-conditioning pathway rather than the temporal architecture alone. Because the ablation removes both U-Net refinement and DAFT, it does not identify which of these components is responsible for the difference, nor does it establish that the temporal transformer is redundant. The same architecture, evaluated in the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT configuration, exhibits the gradual and monotonic degradation that one would expect of a temporally-aware model: each additional dropped input contributes a measurable loss of fidelity, with the largest decrements concentrated in the medial-temporal regions where atrophy is most pronounced. This is consistent with two complementary readings: (i) clinically, SynBADD remains usable even when the longitudinal record is sparse, because the combined refinement and conditioning pathway provides a reliable prior; (ii) architecturally, the temporal transformer is not the dominant contributor to imputation quality in the conditioned configuration, but it does encode useful information that the unconditioned ablation actively exploits.

% ----------------------------------------------------------
% 5.3.2 Effect of Asymmetric Context Availability
% ----------------------------------------------------------
\subsubsection{Effect of Asymmetric Context Availability}
\label{sec:asymmetry}

\paragraph{Setup.} For each subject in the CN and MCI cohorts we fix the target as the central timepoint (m12), and define four single-input mask configurations: \textit{drop\_m06} (past, $-6$ months from target), \textit{drop\_m18} (MCI only; near future, $+6$ months), \textit{drop\_m24} (mid future, $+12$ months), and \textit{drop\_m36} (far future, $+24$ months). We additionally include a \textit{full} configuration that retains every available input. The AD cohort is excluded from this experiment because no AD subject has an m36 scan, which makes the asymmetry test degenerate (only $\pm 12$-month inputs are available). The combination of past-vs-future and near-vs-far contrasts allows us to disentangle a directional bias from a recency effect.

\paragraph{Past versus future.} Across both models and both diagnostic groups, masking the single past input ($-$m06) yields metric distributions that are statistically indistinguishable from masking the single near-future input. A paired Wilcoxon test of SSIM under past-mask versus the average future-mask gives $p = 0.92$ for the UNet$+$DAFT model and $p = 0.35$ for the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model, both far above the $\alpha = 0.05$ threshold. This is consistent across CN ($p = 1.00$ and $p = 0.64$) and MCI ($p = 0.84$ and $p = 0.48$). The bidirectional architectural choice does not produce direction-biased behaviour in either model. Figure~\ref{fig:exp2_past_vs_future} illustrates this in scatter form: the EPE under past-mask and under (mean) future-mask are tightly clustered around the line $y=x$ for the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model on the MCI cohort.

\begin{figure}[ht]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{images/exp2_past_vs_future_scatter.pdf}
    \caption{Per-subject EPE under past-input mask versus mean future-input mask, MCI cohort, No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model (Exp.~2). The scatter is symmetric around $y=x$; no direction bias is detectable.}
    \label{fig:exp2_past_vs_future}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Near versus far.} The recency effect is the cleaner of the two contrasts. Figure~\ref{fig:exp2_metrics} shows PSNR and EPE per mask configuration for both models. The UNet$+$DAFT model is again essentially flat (per-group SSIM range $\leq 5 \times 10^{-8}$). The No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model shows a small but consistent increase in EPE---and a corresponding small drop in PSNR---when any single input is masked. Crucially, the per-subject Spearman correlation between the masked input's distance from the target (in months) and the resulting EPE is $\rho = -0.29$ ($p = 6.96 \times 10^{-4}$, $n = 60$) for the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model, with $72\%$ of subjects exhibiting a negative correlation. The corresponding correlation for the UNet$+$DAFT model is $\rho = +0.10$ ($p = 0.57$). In other words, the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model is more degraded when a temporally proximal input is masked than when a distant one is removed, which is precisely the recency-weighted aggregation behaviour one would hope for.

    \label{fig:exp2_metrics}
\end{figure}

    \caption{EPE versus the absolute distance (in months) of the masked input from the target, for both models (Exp.~2). The UNet$+$DAFT model is flat; the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model shows a clean recency effect---closer inputs matter more.}
    \label{fig:exp2_recency}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Region-level effects.} A per-ROI breakdown of Dice under the four mask configurations is reported in the supplementary tables. For the UNet$+$DAFT model the within-cohort range across configurations is at most $9 \times 10^{-3}$ Dice in any ROI. For the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model the changes are larger but still modest---typically $5 \times 10^{-3}$--$1 \times 10^{-2}$ Dice---and concentrated in the entorhinal cortex and the temporal-lobe regions. The third ventricle and amygdala, both of which are structurally well-defined and large relative to the per-input deformation, are the most stable.

\paragraph{Interpretation.} The asymmetric-context experiment provides three findings. First, neither model exhibits a past-versus-future bias: the bidirectional architectural choice does not produce direction-biased behaviour in either trained instance. Second, the No-UNet$+$No-DAFT model exhibits a weak but statistically robust recency effect---closer inputs are more informative than distant ones, with effect size strongest for MCI subjects. Third, the UNet$+$DAFT model is statistically invariant under all single-input masks, again consistent with the interpretation that the combined refinement and conditioning pathway can reduce sensitivity to the temporal inputs in the trained main configuration. Together with the reduced-input experiment in Section~\ref{sec:reduced_inputs}, this paints a coherent picture: SynBADD's temporal transformer architecture \emph{can} learn time-aware aggregation behaviour, but in the configuration used elsewhere in this paper the combined U-Net and metadata-conditioning pathway is associated with substantially reduced sensitivity to the available longitudinal observations. From the perspective of robustness to missing inputs, this is a useful property: the model degrades gracefully when the longitudinal record is sparse or asymmetric. From the perspective of architectural attribution, however, it reframes the contribution of each component: the combined spatial-refinement and metadata-conditioning pathway is a major driver of imputation quality in the trained main model.
